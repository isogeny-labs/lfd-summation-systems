# First and last fall degree for prime-field index-calculus systems

## Result and scope

This note proves **LFD upper bounds, exact homogeneous-syzygy first-fall
degrees, and a presentation-transfer formula** for direct prime-field PKM
systems. It also gives exact LFDs for an explicit nonsingular-curve family.
It does not establish a constant LFD for arbitrary targets or a cryptographic index-calculus speedup.
No novelty claim is made for these algebraic arguments.

There is now a [partial Lean formalization](../formal/README.md): general
closure soundness, the explicit normal-form section criterion, the graded-lex
finite-quotient theorem and GB bridge, the exact quadratic family, and a
prime-field presentation counterexample. The general PKM finite-quotient instantiation and raw/all-m FFD results
remain written results. The normalized m=2 classification is now
[Lean-checked for every k>=2](IC_GENERAL_PRIME_LEAN.md), including both
ordinary Koszul and truncated-quotient conventions. A growing-depth
order-two-target prime family also has exact normalized LFD 4 in Lean.
The earlier [fixed-k proof](IC_HOMOGENEOUS_FFD_LEAN.md) retains its stronger
coefficient-ring generality.
The [theorem ledger](IC_THEOREM_LEDGER.md) tracks coverage.

For the direct, **normalized PKM subgroup-chain presentation** with m summands
and a coordinate subgroup of size 2^k, the proved bound is

\[
                 d_{\rm LFD}(F_{\rm NF})\le mk+1.
\]

For the raw direct summation-polynomial presentation, a valid bound is

\[
 d_{\rm LFD}(F_{\rm raw})\le
                  \max\{mk+1,\;m2^{m-1}\}.
\]

These are bounds on all elements of the polynomial ideal, not merely on
affine-linear consequences or the repository's measured MutantXL stopping
criterion. The proof does not use sampled degrees, ML predictions,
semi-regularity, enumerated solutions, or a first-fall-degree assumption.

An explicit family at m=2, k=1 has exact LFD 3 after normalization and exact
LFD 4 before normalization. The two presentations generate the same ideal.
Both the affine first-strict-fall convention and the homogeneous-syzygy FFD
convention give 3 and 4, respectively, for this family.

There are also exact **homogeneous-syzygy** FFD results beyond that family:

- For the raw direct system, FFD is exactly m*2^(m-1), for every m>=2 and k>=1.
- For the normalized system with m=2 and k>=2, FFD is 3 when 3*t^2+a=0 and
  4 otherwise, where t is the target x-coordinate.
- For any m, a nonconstant normalized generator has FFD at most
  m*min(k,m)+1, independently of additional, unused chain variables.

Here homogeneous FFD initially means syzygies modulo Koszul syzygies in the
ordinary polynomial ring. For the finite-field truncated-ring convention,
the results hold when p exceeds the stated degree or bound. This condition
is automatic for the exact normalized m=2 result since p>3 and FFD<=4.
These FFD statements do not assert equality with LFD or solving degree.
In fact, an exact normalized m=2 counterexample has both first-fall
conventions equal to 4 and LFD equal to 5; see Section 7 and the
[counterexample certificates](IC_FALL_COUNTEREXAMPLES.md).

The exact FFD statements concern the chains plus **one** summation equation.
Additional liftability filters, distinctness constraints, or auxiliary-variable
equations change the generating list and require a new FFD analysis. The LFD
upper bound still applies to additional normalized constraints in the same
chain variables. A polynomial zero is not by itself a certificate of a useful
rational-point relation; those require the group-law checks used elsewhere
in the repository.

## 1. Definitions and sources

Let K be a field, R=K[z_1,...,z_r], and F a finite generating list. Let V_(F,D)
be the smallest K-vector subspace of R_(<=D) such that:

1. It contains every f in F with deg(f)<=D.
2. If h is in V_(F,D), it contains uh whenever deg(uh)<=D.

The second condition applies also to **newly produced lower-degree rows**.
Static Macaulay rows consisting only of bounded multiples of the original
generators do not in general compute this space.

The last fall degree is

\[
 d_F=\min\{d\ge0: f\in V_{F,\max\{d,\deg f\}}
                           \text{ for every }f\in(F)\}.
\]

The affine first strict fall in this note means the least D with

\[
 V_{F,D}\cap R_{\le D-1}\ne V_{F,D-1}.
\]

Some literature instead defines FFD by nontrivial homogeneous syzygies modulo
Koszul and quotient-induced syzygies. We distinguish those definitions;
agreement for the family below is proved separately.

Specifically, let f_i^top have degree d_i. In the ordinary ring define
d_ff^Kos as the least D admitting a homogeneous syzygy (h_i), with
deg(h_i)+d_i=D, outside the module generated by the Koszul syzygies
f_i^top*e_j-f_j^top*e_i. Set it to infinity if no such syzygy exists.
Section 4 uses this definition.

Caminata--Gorla Definition 1.3 instead works in
B=F_p[z_1,...,z_r]/(z_1^p,...,z_r^p), also declaring
(f_i^top)^(p-1)*e_i trivial. In degrees D<p, the homogeneous components,
syzygies, and trivial syzygies are the same as in the ordinary ring: the
quotient relations and the additional trivial syzygies have higher degree.
Thus a proved value/bound below p transfers to that convention. We do not
silently extend the large raw FFD formula to that convention for small p.

Sources actually read:

- Huang, Kosters, Yang, Yeo, *On the last fall degree of zero-dimensional Weil
  descent systems*, [arXiv:1505.02532v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02532v1),
  Definitions 2.1 and 2.5, Proposition 2.6; its introduction and discussion
  explicitly warn against replacing LFD by FFD.
- Caminata, Gorla, *Solving degree, last fall degree, and related invariants*,
  [arXiv:2112.05579v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05579v2), Definitions 1.3 and
  1.5, Theorem 2.8 and Section 4. Definition 1.3 uses homogeneous syzygies
  modulo trivial syzygies; Definition 1.5 is the closure definition above.
- This repository: `csd/ic_dreg.py`, `pkm_generator`, `prime_profile` and the
  `experiments/ic-dreg` protocol fix the PKM chain presentation. Its rank- and
  semi-regularity-based `d_ff` estimator is not a general implementation of
  every FFD convention in the literature.

## 2. Finite-quotient theorem

**Theorem.** Suppose G is an **explicit subset of F**, is a Groebner basis for
J=(G) for a degree-compatible monomial order, and R/J is finite-dimensional.
Let sigma be the largest total degree of a standard monomial for G, let
delta=max_(f in F) deg(f), and set D=max(sigma+1,delta). Then

\[
                         d_F\le D.
\]

The requirement G subset F matters: the theorem does not give a Groebner basis
for free or ignore the cost/degree of deriving one from arbitrary equations.

**Proof.** Write N for the unique G-normal-form map. Since the order is degree
compatible, division by G never increases total degree. For every h in R,
every reducer multiple used to express h-N(h) has degree at most deg(h).
As G is an input subset, this proves

\[
 h-N(h)\in V_{F,\deg h},\qquad \deg N(h)\le\sigma.       \tag{1}
\]

Let W=V_(F,D) and U=N(W), viewed as a subspace of A=R/J. Equation (1), together
with W's closure and D>=delta, implies that each representative N(w) of U
itself belongs to W. Thus U can be viewed as a subspace of W consisting of
normal forms of degree at most sigma.

For u in U and every variable z_i, the product z_i u has degree at most
sigma+1<=D. Hence z_i u is in W, and (1) gives N(z_i u) in U. Consequently U
is an ideal of the quotient algebra A: it is a vector subspace stable under
multiplication by every algebra generator z_i.

Every original f in F belongs to W because D>=delta. Therefore U contains
N(f) for every f in F and hence the quotient ideal I/J, where I=(F).
Conversely W is contained in I, so U is contained in I/J. Thus

\[
                           U=I/J.                           \tag{2}
\]

For any h in I, (2) puts N(h) in W. Equation (1) puts h-N(h) in V_(F,deg h).
Monotonicity of the V spaces now yields

\[
                         h\in V_{F,\max\{D,\deg h\}}.
\]

This is the defining condition d_F<=D. No radicality, nonempty solution set,
or particular characteristic was used. QED.

**Pure-power corollary.** If G's leading monomials are z_i^(q_i), then the
standard monomials have exponents below q_i, so sigma=sum_i(q_i-1). The bound
is max(1+sum_i(q_i-1),delta). In particular, explicit field equations over
F_q give max(r(q-1)+1,delta), a general finite-field bound, not a special
ECDLP speedup.

## 3. Application to the prime-field PKM chains

Assume p>3 and 2^k divides p-1. In block i, introduce z_(i,j), 0<=j<k, and
the input equations

\[
 z_{i,j}^2-z_{i,j+1}=0\ (j<k-1),\qquad z_{i,k-1}^2-1=0.
\]

They encode z_(i,j)=x_i^(2^j) and x_i^(2^k)=1. There are r=mk variables.
For any degree-compatible order, their leading monomials are the distinct
variable squares. These are pairwise relatively prime, so Buchberger's product
criterion makes the chain equations themselves a Groebner basis G.

The standard monomials are exactly the squarefree monomials. Their largest
degree is sigma=mk, and dim_K(R/J)=2^(mk).

### Normalized presentation

Adjoin g=N(S_(m+1)(z_(1,0),...,z_(m,0),t)) to the chains, or any collection
of already-normalized extra constraints. Every such g has degree at most mk.
The chain equations have degree 2<=mk+1. The theorem proves

\[
                         d_{F_{\rm NF}}\le mk+1.
\]

This works for every target and every coefficient choice for which the stated
presentation is defined, including systems with no solutions.

### Raw presentation

The input degree must be retained when S_(m+1) is not replaced by its normal
form. The summation polynomial has degree at most 2^(m-1) in each variable.
One can see the upper bound by induction from S3: taking the resultant of
S_(n-1) and S3 in their shared variable multiplies old-variable degree bounds
by 2 and gives the same bound for the new variables. After fixing t, total
degree is at most m*2^(m-1). Therefore

\[
 d_{F_{\rm raw}}\le\max\{mk+1,m2^{m-1}\}.
\]

Normalizing has a derivation degree and a computational cost; equality of
ideals does not imply equality of LFDs. The next section gives an exact example.
In split encodings, auxiliary variables must be counted and constrained too;
the direct-system count mk does not silently cover unconstrained auxiliaries.

For k=5, the normalized bounds for m=2,...,9 are respectively
11, 16, 21, 26, 31, 36, 41, 46. These are upper bounds, not measured degrees.

## 4. Exact homogeneous first-fall degrees

### 4.1. The leading form of the raw summation polynomial

Use the usual short-Weierstrass Semaev recursion

\[
 S_{m+1}(x_1,\ldots,x_m,t)
 =\operatorname{Res}_z\bigl(S_m(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1},z),
                           S_3(x_m,t,z)\bigr).
\]

**Lemma.** After fixing a,b,t, the top total-degree form in x_1,...,x_m is

\[
              S_{m+1}^{\rm top}=(x_1\cdots x_m)^{2^{m-1}}.       \tag{5}
\]

In particular the exact raw input degree is delta=m*2^(m-1), for every
choice of these parameters. An invertible scalar normalization of S_n only
rescales the displayed leading form.

**Proof.** Give every coordinate weight 1, a weight 2, and b weight 3.
S3 is weighted homogeneous of weight 4 and has degree 2 in every coordinate.
Inductively, S_n has weight (n-1)*2^(n-2) and degree 2^(n-2) in each
coordinate. The degree bounds follow from the separate homogeneity of the
resultant in the two coefficient lists. Exact degree in a newly introduced
coordinate follows by taking its leading coefficient: it is the square of
the preceding summation polynomial evaluated at the other new coordinate,
since the leading-coordinate coefficient of S3(u,v,z) is (u-z)^2.
Exact degree in an old coordinate also follows from (5) in the induction.

For completeness, if F=S_m has z-degree d=2^(m-2) and weight
W=(m-1)*d, the resultant with S3 has weight

\[
                    2W+4d-2d=2md.
\]

This follows either by scaling z along with the coordinates, or from the
weighted homogeneity of the Sylvester determinant. It proves the asserted
weight induction without assuming a generic coefficient choice.

The base case m=2 of (5) is the term x_1^2*x_2^2 of S3. For the inductive
step, write F(z)=sum_j F_j*z^j and H(z)=S3(x_m,t,z). After fixing a,b,t,
deg_(x_1,...,x_(m-1))(F_j)<=W-j, and every coefficient of H has x_m-degree
at most 2. The resultant is homogeneous of degree 2 in the F_j and degree
d in the coefficients of H. Any monomial involving an F_j with j>0
therefore has total x-degree strictly below 2W+2d=2md.

The only remaining coefficient monomial is F_0^2*lc_z(H)^d. Its coefficient
is 1 in the Sylvester resultant. By induction, the top form of F_0 is
(x_1*...*x_(m-1))^d, while lc_z(H)=(x_m-t)^2. Their product has the unique
top form (x_1*...*x_m)^(2d), with coefficient 1. This proves (5) and ensures
it survives specialization in every characteristic under consideration. QED.

### 4.2. Exact raw FFD for every m

**Theorem.** For the direct raw PKM system, with all chain equations included,

\[
                    d_{\rm ff}^{\rm Kos}(F_{\rm raw})
                              =\delta=m2^{m-1}.               \tag{6}
\]

The same exact value holds for the truncated-ring FFD when p>delta.

**Proof.** The chain top forms are all the variable squares, a regular
sequence. Hence all of their ordinary-ring syzygies are Koszul. Below delta
the extra generator cannot participate in a homogeneous syzygy, so there
are no nontrivial syzygies. At degree delta its top form (5) is divisible
by z_(1,0)^2, one of the chain top forms. The relation

\[
 S_{m+1}^{\rm top}
 -\left(z_{1,0}^{2^{m-1}-2}\prod_{i=2}^m z_{i,0}^{2^{m-1}}\right)
     z_{1,0}^{2}=0
\]

is a syzygy with coefficient 1 in the extra-generator component. In any
Koszul combination, that component lies in the ideal J_top of the chain
squares, which does not contain 1. The syzygy is therefore nontrivial.
This proves (6); the convention transfer follows from delta<p. QED.

This degree reflects the raw generating list, including the work of reducing
the summation equation by chains. It is not a claim that a normalized solver
needs this degree. If the normalized equation is zero, the raw homogeneous
FFD still has the value (6), even though there need not be any affine fall.

### 4.3. A quotient-annihilator criterion

Let J_top=(z_1^2,...,z_r^2), A=R/J_top, and q a nonzero squarefree homogeneous
polynomial of degree e>=1, the top form of a normalized generator. Then

\[
 d_{\rm ff}^{\rm Kos}(z_1^2,\ldots,z_r^2,q)
        = e+\min\{\deg h:0\ne h\in A\text{ homogeneous},\ hq=0\}.       \tag{7}
\]

Indeed, send a syzygy to its q-component modulo J_top. Its image lies in
Ann_A(q), and every element of Ann_A(q) lifts to a syzygy. If that component
lies in J_top, subtract Koszul syzygies involving q to make it zero. What
remains is a syzygy of the chain squares, hence Koszul because they form a
regular sequence. This proves both directions of (7), including the grading.

Unused variables do not change the minimum: the square-zero quotient is a
tensor product with the unused-variable algebra, and multiplication by q
acts only on the active factor. Its kernel is the active annihilator tensored
with that algebra, whose smallest nonzero degree is unchanged.

### 4.4. Exact normalized m=2 FFD for every subgroup depth k>=2

Set u=z_(1,0), v=z_(1,1), w=z_(2,0), z=z_(2,1). The top form of the
normalized S3 equation is

\[
 q=\alpha uw+\beta(uz+vw)+vz,\quad
 \alpha=-2(t^2+a),\quad \beta=-2t.                         \tag{8}
\]

This expression follows directly by replacing x_1^2 by v and x_2^2 by z
in the explicit S3 formula. Its coefficient of vz is 1, so it is always a
nonzero quadratic, irrespective of b and of lower terms.

**Theorem.** For every p>3, nonsingular curve, target x-coordinate t, and
k>=2 satisfying the subgroup condition,

\[
 d_{\rm ff}^{\rm Kos}(F_{\rm NF})=
 \begin{cases}3,&3t^2+a=0,\\4,&3t^2+a\ne0.\end{cases}             \tag{9}
\]

The same value holds for the truncated-ring definition since 4<p.

**Proof.** In the active square-zero quotient, multiplication by q from
degree 1 to degree 3 has matrix, using rows uvw,uvz,uwz,vwz and columns
u,v,w,z,

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}
 \beta&\alpha&0&0\\1&\beta&0&0\\
 0&0&\beta&\alpha\\0&0&1&\beta
 \end{pmatrix},\quad
 \det M=(\alpha-\beta^2)^2=4(3t^2+a)^2.             \tag{10}
\]

There is no constant annihilator because q!=0. If 3t^2+a=0, M is singular,
so a nonzero linear annihilator exists. Explicitly,
q=(v+beta*u)(z+beta*w), and v-beta*u annihilates it modulo the squares.
Equation (7) gives FFD=3.

If 3t^2+a!=0, M is invertible, so no linear annihilator exists. But uv is
a nonzero degree-2 annihilator: every term of q contains u or v. More
explicitly,

\[
 uvq=u^2(\alpha vw+\beta vz)+v^2(\beta uw+uz).
\]

Thus (7) gives FFD=4. Unused chain variables do not change either minimum.
This proves (9) for every k>=2, not just for tested subgroup sizes. QED.

The verification includes E/F_17: y^2=x^3+2x+2 and target (3,1). Both the
curve group and this target have prime order 19; the normalized FFD is 4.
The theorem is not restricted to the special order-two-target family below.

### 4.5. A normalized homogeneous FFD bound for every m

**Corollary.** If the single normalized summation equation is nonconstant,

\[
 d_{\rm ff}^{\rm Kos}(F_{\rm NF})\le m\min(k,m)+1.                \tag{11}
\]

For the truncated-ring definition require p>m*min(k,m)+1.

**Proof.** Each coordinate exponent in S_(m+1) is at most 2^(m-1). If k>=m,
its chain normal form uses only bit positions 0,...,m-1 in each block; if
k<m it uses only the available k positions. Thus the top form q involves
at most s=m*min(k,m) active variables. Let e=deg(q)>=1. Any squarefree
monomial in the active variables of degree s-e+1 is nonzero in their
square-zero quotient, and its product with q has degree s+1, hence is zero.
Equation (7) gives FFD<=e+(s-e+1)=s+1. If the actual support is smaller,
use its size in place of s. QED.

If the normalized generator is zero, omit it; if it is a nonzero constant,
the ideal is the unit ideal. Neither case is covered by (11), and the
Koszul FFD of those homogeneous lists is infinity. A constant-in-k upper
bound for homogeneous FFD is not a constant LFD bound.

## 5. Exact relation between raw and normalized LFD

**Theorem.** Let g=N(S_(m+1)) and delta=m*2^(m-1). If g!=0, then

\[
 d_{F_{\rm raw}}=\max\{\delta,d_{F_{\rm NF}}\}.                 \tag{12}
\]

If g=0, both LFDs are zero.

For a concrete zero-normal-form case take E/F_11: y^2=x^3+2x+1, target
(3,1), and k=1. The curve is nonsingular and S3 vanishes on the entire
{+1,-1}^2 coordinate grid. Its raw homogeneous FFD is 4, while both LFDs
are zero. This also illustrates why the FFD convention matters.

**Proof.** The unique degree-delta raw top monomial in (5) is reducible by a
chain square. Degree-compatible division therefore gives deg(g)<delta and
S-g in V_(G,delta). For every D>=delta each generating list is contained in
the degree-D closure of the other. Minimality of that closure proves

\[
                         V_{F_{\rm raw},D}=V_{F_{\rm NF},D}.       \tag{13}
\]

Below delta the raw equation is unavailable, so V_(F_raw,D) is contained
in the chain ideal J. The nonzero normal form g does not belong to J and
has degree less than delta, proving d_(F_raw)>=delta. Equality (13) and
the defining LFD property give d_(F_raw)<=max(delta,d_(F_NF)); conversely,
any raw LFD at least delta is an upper bound for the normalized LFD by
(13). This proves (12). If g=0, both lists generate J and contain the
degree-compatible Groebner basis G. Normal-form division already derives
every h in J at degree deg(h), so both LFDs are zero. QED.

Combining (12) with the normalized LFD bound gives the exact conditional
result

\[
       g\ne0,\quad m2^{m-1}\ge mk+1
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_{F_{\rm raw}}=m2^{m-1}.       \tag{14}
\]

The nonzero condition is checkable by a single subgroup-grid tuple at which
the summation equation does not vanish. It is automatic if k>=m: then
2^k>2^(m-1), and every raw coordinate exponent is below the chain modulus,
so distinct monomials remain distinct under the bit encoding.

For the requested range at k=5:

| m | Exact raw homogeneous FFD | Normalized homogeneous FFD upper bound | Normalized LFD upper bound | Exact raw LFD if g!=0 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 4 | 4 (exact generic); 3 if 3t^2+a=0 | 11 | max(4, normalized LFD) |
| 3 | 12 | 10 | 16 | max(12, normalized LFD) |
| 4 | 32 | 17 | 21 | 32 |
| 5 | 80 | 26 | 26 | 80 |
| 6 | 192 | 31 | 31 | 192 |
| 7 | 448 | 36 | 36 | 448 |
| 8 | 1024 | 41 | 41 | 1024 |
| 9 | 2304 | 46 | 46 | 2304 |

The FFD columns use the ordinary-ring convention. For the truncated-ring
convention use p greater than the respective bound/value; p>2304 suffices
for every entry in this table. Normalized FFD bounds require a nonconstant
normalized equation. These are proved algebraic invariants of the specified
presentations, not timing measurements or certificates of useful relations.

## 6. An exact nonsingular-curve family

Take p>3, a in F_p with a!=0,+1,-1, and

\[
 E:y^2=x^3+ax,\quad R=(0,0),\quad V=\{1,-1\},\quad m=2,k=1.
\]

The curve is nonsingular since 4a^3!=0. R is a rational point of order two.
This is a deliberately special empty relation-finding family, not a generic
prime-order cryptographic-target family.

With x,y the two factor-base coordinates, the raw S3 equation at target zero is

\[
 S=x^2y^2-2axy+a^2.
\]

Write Cx=x^2-1, Cy=y^2-1. A degree-4 chain reduction gives

\[
 S-y^2 Cx-Cy=1+a^2-2axy=-2a(xy-c),\quad c=(1+a^2)/(2a).  \tag{3}
\]

Our hypothesis ensures

\[
                    c^2-1=(a^2-1)^2/(4a^2)\ne0.           \tag{4}
\]

Let h=xy-c. Define the following explicit degree-3 derivations:

\[
 A=yh-xCy=x-cy,\qquad B=xh-yCx=y-cx.
\]

Then A+cB=(1-c^2)x. By (4), x lies in V_(F_NF,3). Multiplying this newly
derived linear row by x stays within degree 3; subtracting Cx yields 1.
Thus the ideal is the unit ideal and 1 belongs to V_(F_NF,3).

For a unit ideal, the least D at which 1 belongs to V_(F,D) is exactly its
last fall degree: 1 gives all polynomials at their allowed degree, and its
absence at a smaller D contradicts the defining LFD condition.

In the normalized presentation F_NF={Cx,Cy,h}, V_(F_NF,2) is just their span:
the leading forms x^2,y^2,xy are linearly independent, so the span has no
nonzero element of degree below 2 and cannot grow by allowed nonconstant
multiplication. In particular 1 is absent. Consequently

\[
                         d_{F_{\rm NF}}=3.
\]

For F_raw={Cx,Cy,S}, the generator S is unavailable below degree 4. Hence
V_(F_raw,3) is contained in (Cx,Cy); evaluating at (1,1) shows 1 is absent.
At degree 4, equation (3) constructs h and the preceding degree-3 derivation
constructs 1 without exceeding 4. Therefore

\[
                         d_{F_{\rm raw}}=4.
\]

### First-fall conventions for this family

For the affine strict-fall convention, the normalized degree-2 span has no
lower rows, and at degree 3 it produces x, so the first fall is 3. For the raw
presentation, the chain-only system has no falls through degree 3 (it is a
degree-compatible Groebner basis); equation (3) produces a degree-2 polynomial
at degree 4 that is outside the chain ideal. Thus its first strict fall is 4.

For homogeneous syzygies modulo trivial syzygies, normalized top forms have
the degree-3 syzygy x*(xy)-y*(x^2)=0. There is no degree-2 syzygy, and Koszul
syzygies first occur in degree 4, so FFD=3. Raw top forms x^2,y^2,x^2y^2 have
no syzygy below 4, and the degree-4 syzygy y^2*(x^2)-1*(x^2y^2)=0 is not a
Koszul combination: its third coefficient is a nonzero constant, while Koszul
syzygies involving the third generator have degree at least 6. Thus FFD=4.
For p>3, quotient relations x^p=y^p=0 do not affect these degree-3/4 arguments.

## 7. First and last fall degree can differ

For E/F_2689: y^2=x^3+1739x+1574, target (797,1184), and k=6, the direct
normalized m=2 system has homogeneous-syzygy FFD=4, affine first strict
fall=4, and exact LFD=5. The curve is nonsingular, the target has prime order
1307, and its x-coordinate is outside the coordinate subgroup of size 64.
The strict separation disproves universal equality of these invariants and
the universal bound LFD<=FFD, even within this one prime-field encoding.

The [full counterexample note](IC_FALL_COUNTEREXAMPLES.md) gives the explicit
input, an ordinary-degree-preserving quotient-closure lemma, a closed-subspace
certificate excluding 1 at degree 4, and an exact replay deriving 1 at degree 5.
An independent ordinary-ring implementation also checks the degree-4 lower
bound. A smaller instance over F_257 has exact FFD=3 and LFD=4.

These are finite exact counterexamples. They do not show that LFD is
unbounded as k grows, or disprove a high-probability heuristic allowing
exceptional instances. The general normalized LFD bound in Section 3 and
the exact homogeneous FFD results in Section 4 remain valid.

## 8. Verification and practical limits

`scripts/verify_ic_fall_proofs.py` checks the parameterized identities with
SymPy, then constructs V_(F,D) in the **ordinary polynomial ring** using exact
finite-field elimination and iterative multiplication of newly fallen rows.
It verifies the exact 3/4 values at concrete prime-field specializations and
checks full ideal dimensions for small PKM systems against independent solution
enumeration. It also verifies the four-variable top-form identities and the
generic S4 leading form by an exact resultant. Tests check degree-3 and -4

The quotient already has dimension 2^(mk); the bound grows with k. Thus it is
not a small constant LFD bound and gives no efficient index-calculus algorithm
