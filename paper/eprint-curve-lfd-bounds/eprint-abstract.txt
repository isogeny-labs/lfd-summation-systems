In elliptic-curve index calculus, each attempt to decompose a point over the factor base solves a Semaev summation-polynomial system. Together with the number of solutions, the last fall degree (LFD) of that system governs the cost of linear-algebra solving; it is the degree past which a degree-by-degree computation needs no further degree fall. We treat prime-field curves with two-power subgroup factor bases and binary ordinary and Koblitz curves. In Lean 4, without project axioms, we prove $\mathrm{LFD}\le\max(mk+1,\delta)$ for inputs containing the squaring chains, $\mathrm{LFD}\le k+2$ for two summands on nonsingular curves at every depth $k$, and lower bounds at every depth under explicit nondegeneracy conditions. For two summands and generic targets we certify $\mathrm{LFD}=4$, $5$, $6$ through $k=13$ on a unit instance, matching the degree-of-regularity breakpoints of Petit, Kosters and Messeng; at $k=14$, $\mathrm{LFD}\ge7$ is certified and $\mathrm{LFD}\le7$ rests on a degree lift with a written proof. These values agree with the conjecture $\mathrm{LFD}(k)=\lfloor(k+14)/4\rfloor$. By contrast, the first fall degree of these two-summand prime systems, used as a proxy in parts of the literature, is $3$ or $4$ at every depth $k\ge2$ (kernel-checked), while by written proofs the LFD of unit-ideal instances with target abscissa $t\ne0$ is unbounded in the depth in characteristic $0$ or large characteristic (kernel-checked for a family of curves). The solving degree of two-summand prime systems is at most $k+2$ (written proof). We make no discrete-logarithm cost claim.
