We study the last fall degree (LFD) of summation-polynomial systems for elliptic-curve index calculus in three families: prime-field curves with a two-power subgroup factor base in squaring chains, and binary ordinary and Koblitz curves with Boolean descents. Using Lean 4 and Mathlib, we prove the following bounds without project axioms: $\mathrm{LFD}\le\max(mk+1,\delta)$ for every input list with the chains; $\mathrm{LFD}\le k+2$ for two summands on nonsingular curves at every depth $k$; $\mathrm{LFD}\ge4$ at every depth for unit ideals with $3t^2+a\ne0$; $\mathrm{LFD}\ge7$ for three summands at every $k\ge3$ when $5t^3+3at+6b\ne0$. Exact computation gives a certified sweep of 3005 systems (prime subgroup and coset bases, random and Frobenius-invariant binary bases), and for two summands and generic targets $\mathrm{LFD}=4$ for $k\le5$, $5$ for $k=6,\ldots,9$ and $6$ for $k=10,\ldots,13$ on a unit instance, matching the corrected degree-of-regularity breakpoints of Petit, Kosters and Messeng, whose unreduced presentation has the same LFD there. At $k=14$, the lower bound $\mathrm{LFD}\ge7$ is certified. The matching upper bound comes from a degree lift with a written proof and no certificate; the Lean statements take the recorded closures as hypotheses. A written proof, checked in two reviews, shows that in characteristic $0$ or $p>2^{k+1}$ the LFD of unit-ideal instances with target abscissa $t\ne0$ is unbounded, at least $\lfloor(k-10)/14\rfloor+1$ for $k\ge38$ (in Lean, conditional on a vanishing-sum bound; for $y^2=x^3+ax-(1+a)$ with rational $a\notin\{-3,-3/4,-1,1\}$ and target $(1,0)$, it is kernel-checked over $\mathbb{C}$ and admissible prime fields above an explicit cutoff). The law $\mathrm{LFD}(k)=\lfloor(k+14)/4\rfloor$ is a conjecture that agrees with every tested prediction recorded in advance. With three to eight summands at depth $k\le m-1$ the generator has the full degree $mk$ on $409$ of $412$ instances, and there the kernel bound and a carry criterion checked per instance force $\mathrm{LFD}=mk+1$; at $m=4$, $k=4$, where $\deg g=12$ falls short of $mk=16$, the value is $13$, below the bound $17$. For binary and Koblitz systems we compute exact values through dimension $16$ and reproduce them in separate runs of the same engine. These data refute preregistered laws based on the solution count. By a theorem of Caminata and Gorla the solving degree is the larger of the LFD and the largest degree of the reduced Gröbner basis. On these cube systems we bound the latter by $\log_2$ of the number of solutions, so the solving degree of two-summand prime systems is at most $k+2$; it is determined on all 5813 systems with exact LFD and equals the LFD on all but eight. The results concern polynomial-system degrees; we do not estimate discrete-logarithm costs.
