Index calculus on elliptic curves solves a Semaev summation-polynomial system for every relation, and the cost of each solve depends on the degree a Gröbner-basis or linear-algebra solver must reach. Published estimates assume this degree stays roughly constant as the factor base grows. We test that assumption through the last fall degree (LFD), for prime-field curves with two-power subgroup factor bases and for binary ordinary and Koblitz curves. In Lean 4, using only Lean's standard axioms, we prove $\mathrm{LFD}\le k+2$ for two-summand prime-field systems on nonsingular curves at every depth $k\ge2$. Exact computation certifies $\mathrm{LFD}=4$ for $k\le5$ and $5$ for $6\le k\le9$ on random instances, and $6$ for $10\le k\le13$ on a unit instance over $\mathbb{F}_{65537}$; at $k=14$ we certify $\mathrm{LFD}\ge7$, with $\mathrm{LFD}\le7$ resting on a degree lift with a written proof. These values match the corrected breakpoints of Petit, Kosters and Messeng and the law $\mathrm{LFD}(k)=\lfloor(k+14)/4\rfloor$, fitted on $k\le13$. A written proof shows that the LFD of unit-ideal instances with $t\ne0$, which equals their solving degree, is unbounded in $k$ when $p>2^{k+1}$. The degree therefore grows with the factor base, though slowly. For the ECDLP this bounds the cost of these attacks from below: in the two-summand relation search of Petit, Kosters and Messeng, the attempts that find no relation each cost a Macaulay-matrix solver an amount exponential in $k$, so complexity estimates that assume a bounded solver degree understate the cost of the relation search by a factor exponential in $k$. For the direct presentation with $m\ge3$ summands, a written proof with a kernel-checked core gives $\mathrm{LFD}\ge m\min(k,m-1)+1$ under explicit nondegeneracy conditions, and a two-summand slice extends the unbounded growth to unit-ideal instances with any fixed number of summands.
