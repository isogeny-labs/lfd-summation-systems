We study the last fall degree (LFD) of summation-polynomial systems for elliptic-curve index calculus in three families: prime-field curves with a two-power subgroup factor base in squaring chains, and binary ordinary and Koblitz curves with Boolean descents. In Lean 4 with Mathlib, without project axioms, we kernel-check $\mathrm{LFD}\le\max(mk+1,\delta)$ for every input list with the chains; $\mathrm{LFD}\le k+2$ for two summands on nonsingular curves at every depth $k$; $\mathrm{LFD}\ge4$ at every depth for unit ideals with $3t^2+a\ne0$; $\mathrm{LFD}\ge7$ for three summands at every $k\ge3$ when $5t^3+3at+6b\ne0$. Exact computation gives a certified sweep of 3005 systems (prime subgroup and coset bases, random and Frobenius-invariant binary bases), and for two summands and generic targets $\mathrm{LFD}=4$ for $k\le5$, $5$ for $k=6,\ldots,9$ and $6$ for $k=10,\ldots,13$ on a unit instance, matching the corrected degree-of-regularity breakpoints of Petit, Kosters and Messeng, whose unreduced presentation has the same LFD there. At $k=14$, $\mathrm{LFD}\ge7$ is certified and $\mathrm{LFD}\le7$ rests on a degree lift with a written proof and no certificate; in Lean these values are conditional on recorded closures. A twice-reviewed written proof shows that in characteristic $0$ or $p>2^{k+1}$ the LFD of unit-ideal instances with target abscissa $t\ne0$ is unbounded, at least $\lfloor(k-10)/14\rfloor+1$ for $k\ge38$ (in Lean, conditional on a vanishing-sum bound; for the curve $y^2=x^3+6x-7$ with the target of order two the bound is kernel-checked without that hypothesis, over $\mathbb{C}$ and along an increasing sequence of primes). The law $\mathrm{LFD}(k)=\lfloor(k+14)/4\rfloor$ is a conjecture that agrees with every tested prediction recorded in advance. With three to eight summands at depth $k\le m-1$ the generator has the full degree $mk$ on $409$ of $412$ instances, and there the kernel bound and a carry criterion checked per instance force $\mathrm{LFD}=mk+1$; at $m=4$, $k=4$, where $\deg g=12$ falls short of $mk=16$, the value is $13$, below the bound $17$. Binary and Koblitz values are exact through dimension $16$ (replicated, one engine); preregistered laws in the solution count are refuted. We claim nothing about discrete-logarithm cost.
